# Search and evidence audit

## Review scope and source selection

The source draft supplied seven randomized trials and an account of a short general web search. Its full query list and screening history were unavailable. On November 11, 2026, targeted web and scholarly-index searches checked those records and looked for additional trials and reports. Search results from Consensus and SciSpace were used as leads, followed by checking journal, repository, PubMed, and primary full-text records. The Elicit request returned no usable results. The update added the randomized pilot of gender dialogue plus savings in Côte d’Ivoire (Gupta et al., 2013) and the pregnancy-focused trial in Ethiopia (Agde et al., 2025). The nine core trials span seven countries.

The revision uses reported IPV as a core eligibility criterion. It codes joint delivery by what the intervention offered, rather than inferring that delivery was joint because a trial recruited or randomized couples. The Côte d’Ivoire program offered dialogue groups jointly to women and their male partners. Some women in the wider program could invite a male relative instead. The extraction uses the study’s analysis of partnered women. The Ethiopian pregnancy program invited pregnant women and spouses to the same education program and recorded couple attendance. Its follow-up took place after delivery and the endpoint concerns violence during pregnancy.

The Bandebereho six-year follow-up and mechanism paper belong to the same randomization. The Indashyikirwa community activism evaluation is contextual evidence with a different exposure and unit. The 2025 report on men’s knowledge, attitudes, and controlling behaviors is linked to the Hadiya trial and adds no trial to the count. The Darbes HIV testing trial is contextual because its main outcomes are HIV testing and sexual risk. Zambia’s CETA trial enrolled couples and treated participants separately. The Rwanda refugee agency trial and the Tanzania TEVAW study also engaged both partners in separate activities. Joint training of facilitators in the former is distinct from delivery to participants. These examples were checked as potential inclusions.

The method is a targeted narrative update. No exhaustive search, duplicate screening, formal risk-of-bias rating, or protocol registration is claimed.

## Endpoint and comparison checks

- **Indashyikirwa.** The abstract and the women’s results identify the 24-month adjusted risk ratio as 0.44, with a 95% confidence interval of 0.34 to 0.59. Another value appears in the discussion. The revised text uses the designated results. The figure uses odds ratios only, so this risk ratio appears in Table 2 and the narrative.
- **Bandebereho.** The 2023 paper reports the 21-month and six-year estimates side by side in its women’s outcome table. The adjusted odds ratios for women’s reported physical IPV are 0.37 and 0.45, respectively. The six-year analysis includes 1,021 women and reports follow-up loss and changes in some partnerships. The two estimates share a randomization and are not independent replications.
- **Gender dialogue and savings.** Gupta et al. report an odds ratio of 0.92 for the primary past-year physical or sexual IPV outcome in the assignment-based analysis. The interval is 0.58 to 1.47. The economic abuse estimate of 0.39 [0.25, 0.60] refers to a different, secondary outcome. The comparison for highly adherent couples was not used as the primary randomized estimate.
- **Unite for a Better Life.** The main results compare each intervention arm with control. The couple arm’s estimates are 1.00 [0.77, 1.30] for physical IPV and 0.86 [0.62, 1.20] for sexual IPV. A finding in the men-only arm against control does not establish a difference between it and the couple arm.
- **Pregnancy-focused education.** The adjusted odds ratio for any IPV during pregnancy is 0.259 [0.161, 0.417] against standard care. The intervention and endpoint are specific to pregnancy. The point estimate and limits are rounded to two decimal places in the figure only.
- **Couples Health CoOp.** The reported odds ratio of 0.38 [0.20, 0.74] compares the joint arm with the gender-separate arm for the favorable outcome of no victimization. The figure excludes this contrast because its endpoint direction and comparator differ from the plotted results. The manuscript explains the direction and the restriction to participants remaining in the same relationship.
- **Ibadan and Becoming One.** The review preserves the original score and absolute probability-difference scales, respectively. It does not convert these to odds ratios or reconstruct confidence intervals from the supplied draft’s standard errors.
- **Mlambe.** Its effects were reported in a small pilot sample recruited from HIV care clinics. The evidence map retains the pilot as a separate trial and qualifies inferences about replication and transfer.

## Safety and interpretation

No trial-level effect is interpreted as approval for joint work in relationships marked by coercive control or serious risk. The paper draws the clinical and research safety implications from cited WHO guidance and the literature on relational intervention. The trials largely reflect different-sex relationships and women’s violence reports. Mechanism and subgroup analyses are treated according to their designs. Trial interventions differ enough that the revision gives no pooled estimate and does not rank programs by numerical effect size.
